# If hired: what changes, and how

**Role:** Lead Security Engineer, Core Technology Team (APT, Stockholm — on-site).

`01-day-one-operating-plan.md` is the *how* of executing. This is the *what is
different afterwards* — the commitments I would make, and how you would check
whether I met them.

**A caveat that is not false modesty.** Every target below assumes things I
cannot verify from outside: that AWS org-level control exists or can be
established, that legal/DPO engagement is possible, that engineering leadership
will back an unpopular call at least occasionally. If those are absent, the
timeline moves and I would say so in month one rather than in month nine.

---

## The one-sentence version

**Within twelve months, security at APT becomes something the engineering
organisation can demonstrate rather than assert** — controls that hold without
being remembered, evidence produced by pipelines rather than by people, and an
AI-agent data path that is bounded by authorisation instead of by policy.

## What I would commit to

### By day 30 — the bleeding stops

| Outcome | How you check it |
|---|---|
| A written baseline assessment of the current security posture exists and has been circulated | The document; whether people argue with it |
| Long-lived AWS access keys are inventoried, with a dated plan to eliminate them | Key age report, trending |

| An interim AI-usage guidance page exists | One page. Not a policy binder. |
| Nothing has been re-architected | Deliberate. Month-one re-architecture proposals are how new security leads spend credibility they have not earned. |

### By day 90 — controls exist and are exercised

| Outcome | How you check it |
|---|---|
| SCPs prevent the worst outcomes (root use, disabling CloudTrail/GuardDuty, region sprawl) | Try one in a sandbox account |
| Every production IAM role that touches regulated data has a permission boundary | Count roles with boundaries ÷ total |
| One tabletop exercise run, with legal in the room, on the notification decision | The exercise report, including what it found wrong |
| Detection rules live in version control with false-positive budgets | The repository |
| AI-agent data access is instrumented — who read what, when | An audit record you can query |
| A decision log exists with real entries, including accepted risks | `docs/adr/`, with dates |

### By month 12 — it holds without me

| Outcome | Target | Why this metric and not a flattering one |
|---|---|---|
| Findings past SLA | **0**, enforced in CI | A build metric, not a spreadsheet metric — it cannot be massaged |
| Expired risk exceptions | **0** | An acceptance that renews silently is an allowlist |
| Production workloads under IaC guardrails | **> 95%** | Counting only workloads that matter |
| Long-lived database credentials | **0** — IAM auth everywhere | Removes the most common breach path rather than managing it |
| Detection coverage vs ATT&CK Cloud | Mapped, **with gaps named** | A map with honest gaps beats a claim of 90% |
| Compliance evidence | Generated by pipeline | Art. 30 and ISO 27001 evidence as build output, not a quarterly scramble |
| Security champions with allocated time | **1 per team** | Without allocated time it is a title |
| Controls only I can explain | **Trending to 0** | The real test of whether this survives my leaving |

## The three things I would actually be judged on

Everything above is measurable. These are the ones that matter, and only the
first is easy to measure.

**1. The AI-agent data path is safe by construction.**
This is the differentiating requirement in the posting and the thing I have
already built a working reference for. Concretely: no agent reads unmasked
personal data, agent identities cannot write, every access is attributable, and
the controls are demonstrable on demand rather than described. This repository is
the pattern; applying it to APT's actual estate is the work.

**2. Security stops being a bottleneck and becomes a guardrail.**
The measurable proxy is the ratio of guardrails shipped to gates added. The
honest signal is whether teams still invite me to design discussions in month
nine. If they have stopped, I have become a gate and the approach has failed —
regardless of what the control coverage says.

**3. The organisation can answer a regulator without a fire drill.**
"Which agent read what health data, when, and under what lawful basis" should be
a query, not a project. That is the difference between compliance as evidence and
compliance as archaeology.

## What I would *not* promise

Being specific, because a candidate who promises everything is telling you
something about how they will handle the first hard trade-off:

- **Not zero incidents.** Anyone promising that is either lying or planning to hide them. The commitment is that incidents are detected quickly, contained cleanly, and produce a change.
- **Not full ISO 27001 certification in year one** unless it is already underway and resourced. Certification is a programme with a budget, not a side effect of good engineering.
- **Not an EDR rollout in the first six months.** It is my weakest area (see `00-jd-coverage-matrix.md`), and I would rather select it carefully in month nine than badly in month two.
- **Not immediate fluency in Go and .NET.** I would be reading them before writing them. For a security lead, reading code critically matters more — but the ramp is real and I would not pretend otherwise.
- **Not a security function that never says no.** One that never says no is not doing the job. The commitment is that a no comes with a reason, an alternative, and a written record when the decision goes the other way.

## Why me for this specific role

Not a general claim of competence — the fit argument, narrowly:

- **The posting's differentiating requirement is the thing I have built.** "Secure practices for coding with AI assistants, avoiding data leakage" is an authorisation problem, and this repository is a working, tested, evidenced answer to it. Most candidates will bring a policy document.
- **Regulated data as an engineering constraint, not paperwork.** Consent enforced as a row-level security policy rather than a `WHERE` clause is what GDPR Art. 25 actually asks for.
- **Evidence over assertion.** Every claim in this repository regenerates with one command, including two of my own bugs. That habit is what a security function needs when an auditor or a regulator asks.
- **I name my gaps.** Four of them, in writing, before being asked. That is the same discipline as reporting a control that failed rather than the ones that worked.

## How I would want to be held to this

Quarterly, against the table above, in writing. If a target slips, the useful
question is whether the target was wrong or the execution was — and I would
rather answer that at month three than defend a number at month twelve.

The failure signals in `01-day-one-operating-plan.md` are the early warning:
teams stopping inviting me to design discussions, findings rising while
remediation does not, controls only I can explain, and the tabletop being
rescheduled twice. Any of those appearing means the approach needs changing, not
defending.
